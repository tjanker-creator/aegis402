So a client-signed application call is a legal member of a payment group —
      and atomicity turns it into a precondition of the payment.

  


  
  

    
The problem, in one sentence

    
An agent's reasoning layer is also its payment-authorization layer.

    
Give it a wallet, and the thing that decides is the thing that signs.

    
Every spending limit that lives inside the agent lives inside the blast radius.

  


  
  

    
Not our finding

    
Published in February. Formalised in May.

    
8 Feb 2026 — x402 v2 security review, risk #1: the receiving address comes from the server.
Recommended mitigation: a recipient allowlist.

    
arXiv 2605.11781 — Five Attacks on x402. The malicious payee is explicitly out of scope. No on-chain enforcement proposed.

  


  
  

    
So the question is not whether. It is where.

    

      
inside the agent

        
Every x402 client we found. Same process, same memory, same prompt.

        
attacker is already here

      
inside the payment

        
An immutable contract that co-signs the transfer. Nothing the agent runs can reach it.

    

    
Others enforce elsewhere — LogicSig mandates, ARC-58 plugins, session keys.
      The question is only where the check has to survive.

The question that decides whether any of this is worth anything

    
Who put that address on the allowlist?

    

      merchant's own domain──TLS──▸zkTLS attestor──signs──▸Algorand contract
    

    
If the answer is we did, we have replaced one trust anchor with another —
      and nothing else in this pitch is worth your time.

  


  


  
  

    
Two things I want to be exact about

    

      
not: “a zero-knowledge proof, verified on chain”

      
the ledger recovers an ECDSA signature — ecdsa_pk_recover, secp256k1

    

    

      
not: “this address belongs to the merchant you meant”

      
it proves the domain published the address. Naming is a separate layer, and we do not claim it.

    

    
The guard's own allowlist is still a compiled-in
      constant — it does not read the registry box. It does not have to: the proof rides in the group.
